## Introduction

Attracting Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) into the manufacturing sector has been a central pillar of India's economic strategy aimed at boosting exports and employment. This paper evaluates the trajectory of these investments from 2014 to 2024, assessing the impact of recent policy interventions.

## Sectoral Concentration

Data from the Reserve Bank of India indicates a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 8% in manufacturing FDI over the decade <a id="cite-1" href="#ref-1">[1]</a>. However, this growth is highly asymmetric.

<div class="terminology-callout">
  <strong>Production-Linked Incentive (PLI):</strong> A government scheme that offers companies incentives on incremental sales from products manufactured in domestic units, designed to boost domestic manufacturing and attract large investments.
</div>

Over 65% of the inflows have been directed toward just three sub-sectors: electronics manufacturing (specifically mobile assembly), automotive components, and pharmaceuticals. Traditional labor-intensive sectors such as textiles and leather have seen stagnant or declining foreign investment.

## Impact of Policy Interventions

The introduction of the PLI scheme marked a distinct shift in industrial policy. Our analysis shows a clear inflection point in FDI commitments following the scheme's rollout <a id="cite-2" href="#ref-2">[2]</a>. While the scheme has successfully integrated Indian facilities into global value chains (GVCs) for final assembly, evidence of deep backward integration and technology transfer remains sparse.

## Conclusion

To maximize the long-term economic benefits of FDI, future iterations of industrial policy must incentivize R&D investments and foster stronger linkages between foreign subsidiaries and domestic MSME suppliers.
