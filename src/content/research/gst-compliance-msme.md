## Introduction

The Goods and Services Tax (GST) framework was introduced to streamline indirect taxation in India. While large corporations have largely adapted, micro and small enterprises (MSMEs) continue to face significant structural challenges <a id="cite-1" href="#ref-1">[1]</a>. This paper examines the compliance burden at the district level, focusing on the financial and operational costs incurred by firms with an annual turnover of less than ₹5 Crores.

## Methodology

We conducted a stratified survey of 1,200 MSMEs across 50 districts in five states (Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, and Bihar). The survey collected data on monthly compliance costs, including software subscriptions, accounting fees, and internal labor hours dedicated to tax filing.

## Findings

Our analysis reveals a stark contrast in the relative burden of compliance. For large firms, compliance costs average 0.2% of annual revenue. However, for micro-enterprises, these costs can reach up to 4% <a id="cite-2" href="#ref-2">[2]</a>. The complexity of filing multiple returns, matching invoices for Input Tax Credit (ITC), and responding to frequent regulatory notices constitutes a significant drain on resources.

Furthermore, digital infrastructure disparities exacerbate the problem in semi-urban and rural districts, where internet connectivity and access to trained accountants remain persistent hurdles <a id="cite-3" href="#ref-3">[3]</a>.

## Policy Recommendations

To mitigate these challenges, we propose the following interventions:
1. **Simplified Quarterly Filing:** Expanding the composition scheme to include a broader range of service providers and increasing the threshold limit.
2. **Automated ITC Matching:** Enhancing the GSTN portal's capabilities to provide real-time, automated reconciliation of invoices with minimal manual intervention.
3. **Targeted Subsidies:** Providing subsidies for GST compliance software for registered micro-enterprises.
