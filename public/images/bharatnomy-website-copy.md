# Bharatnomy — Website Copy Draft

*Tone: accessible/journalistic. Audience: the broad informed public and investors. Non-partisanship is shown, not stated.*

---

## Tagline options

1. **"India's economy, examined honestly."**
   Plain, confident, does the positioning work in five words.
2. **"Where policy meets proof."**
   Signals the interdisciplinary method (law + economics + policy + finance) without spelling it out.
3. **"Beyond the headline. Into the system."**
   Directly gestures at the media-fragmentation thesis, more editorial in feel.
4. **"Research built to be used."**
   Leans into open-access, public-utility positioning — good if you want to emphasize accessibility over rigor.
5. **"Understanding India, one system at a time."**
   Warmer, more narrative; works well if the brand voice leans explanatory rather than clinical.

My pick: **#1 or #3** — both are short enough to sit under a nav logo and don't oversell before you have a publication history. #2 is a strong second-place if you want the interdisciplinary method to be the very first thing people register.

## 2. About Page

**Opening (mission, in plain language)**

> Bharatnomy is an independent research firm studying India's economy as a system — where climate, energy, capital markets, and public policy are treated as connected, because they are.
>
> We started Bharatnomy because most research on India's economy is built to serve one audience: other specialists. We're building ours for a wider one — investors, civil servants, journalists, and anyone who wants to understand how the country actually works, not just what happened this week.

**How we think (the core bet, in narrative form — no jargon like "systems-level")**

> Siloed analysis misses how India's economy actually behaves. A heatwave is a climate story, an energy story, an agriculture story, and a fiscal story, all at once — and research that only tells one of those stories tells an incomplete one. Our work is built across disciplines from the start: law, economics, policy, and finance, in the same room, on the same questions.
>
> We're independent, and we intend to stay that way. That shows up in how we work, not in a mission statement — in what we choose to study, who funds it, and how plainly we're willing to write it.

## 3. Research Page

**Header**

> **Research**
> We're early. Here's how we work, and what's coming.

**Intro**

> Bharatnomy doesn't have a publication history yet — we're a new firm, and we'd rather tell you that directly than pretend otherwise. What we do have is a method, a set of questions we think are underexamined, and a first project already underway.

**Our approach**

> Every piece of research we publish will sit in one (or more) of five areas: climate adaptation and resilience, energy policy and markets, India's macroeconomic health, equity markets, and commodity markets. We treat these as connected, not separate — a paper on energy policy will often touch fiscal capacity; a paper on climate resilience will often touch capital markets.
>
> Our output will take three forms:
> - **Working papers** — the deep, technical version, for readers who want the full method.
> - **Reports** — longer-form, structured analysis meant to stand on its own.
> - **Policy briefs** — short, direct, built for people who need the conclusion and the "so what" fast.

**Currently in progress**

> **Tracking Heat Action Plans across Maharashtra**
> India's districts are increasingly required to have Heat Action Plans — but having a plan and implementing one are different things. We're building a district-by-district picture of what's on paper versus what's actually happening on the ground. Still in the research phase — more soon.

**Closing line**

> Want to know when our first paper is out? [Subscribe] — we'll only email when there's something real to share.

## 6. Editorial Standards Page (draft — new page recommendation)

**Header**

> **How we work**
> The standards behind everything we publish.

**Draft content**

> **Independence.** Bharatnomy is not affiliated with any political party, government body, or advocacy organization. We choose our research questions based on what we think matters, not on what serves a particular outcome.
>
> **Methodology, shown not just claimed.** Every working paper and report will include a clear account of our data sources and methods — including their limitations. If a number is uncertain, we'll say so, rather than presenting false precision.
>
> **Sourcing.** We rely on primary data wherever possible — government data, original surveys, and direct fieldwork — and we cite our sources plainly enough that a reader can check our work themselves.

> **Corrections.** If we get something wrong, we'll say so publicly, with a visible correction note, not a silent edit.

## Additional suggestions

- **Contact page or footer contact block** — at minimum: a general inquiries email, and ideally separate lines for media/press and collaboration/data requests. Investors and journalists will look for this specifically.
- **Newsletter capture** — I've placed CTAs on the landing, research, datasets, and dashboards pages. For a pre-launch firm, this list is arguably your most valuable asset right now — worth making it slightly more prominent than a footnote.
- **A short "Cite us" or "Republish" note**, once you have real output — open-access, public-utility positioning is more credible if you explicitly invite reuse (with attribution) rather than leaving it implied.
- **Funding/ownership disclosure** — flagged above under Editorial Standards, but worth deciding early. It's the detail most likely to be asked about by journalists and most likely to build (or cost) trust with a skeptical reader.
